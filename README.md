# SwarKavach

Voice-clone fraud call detection for Hindi-English telephony. The system looks
at a phone call two ways at once, asks whether the voice is real and whether
the conversation is a scam, and combines the two into one calibrated risk score
with evidence attached.

**BCSE419L, Introduction to Natural Language Processing (Speech and Language
Processing Lab), Lab Assessment 2.** Bhavye Nijhawan, 23BAI0100.

---

## The problem with one-eyed defences

Anti-spoofing research reads the waveform. It can tell you a voice was
generated by a machine, and it is very good at that, but a synthetic voice is
not a crime. Bank IVR is synthetic. Accessibility software is synthetic.
Appointment reminders are synthetic.

Text scam classifiers read the words. They can tell you a message is trying to
extract an OTP, and they are defeated the moment a human being says the same
thing in a phrasing nobody wrote down, or a cloned voice reads a script that
sounds perfectly ordinary.

Each is blind exactly where the other sees. The interesting question is what
happens when you put them together, and the answer turns out to be more than
averaging two numbers.

## Four cells

Every call falls into one of four boxes, and the whole evaluation is built
around them.

|                     | Benign script                        | Scam script                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| **Human voice**     | ordinary call, must stay quiet       | human scammer, audio branch misses it |
| **Cloned voice**    | legitimate robocall, audio branch false-alarms | the case everyone worries about |

The two off-diagonal cells are where single-branch systems fail, and they are
the reason the corpus deliberately over-samples them.

## What is actually new here

Late fusion of two scores is not novel and this project does not claim it is.
Five things are.

**Prosody-Intent Mismatch.** A cloned voice reading a threat script says
frightening things in a flat voice. The system measures lexical pressure per
turn from a weighted fraud lexicon, measures acoustic arousal from pitch
movement, syllable emphasis and speaking-rate variation, and scores the gap.
Neither branch can compute this alone, which is what makes it a genuine
cross-modal feature rather than a late-fusion input. On the same words, a flat
synthetic delivery scores about ten times higher than an animated human one.

The offline audio is generated, and the generator had to be taught to make
cloned speech prosodically flat before this feature had anything to detect.
So the offline result shows the method works given the phenomenon; it does not
prove the phenomenon. That distinction is spelled out in `docs/REPORT.md`
section 4.2.1, and testing the premise on real recordings is what the Colab
notebooks are for.

**Coercion trajectory.** A scam call has a shape: claim an authority, state a
problem, threaten, attach a deadline, isolate the victim, extract. Each turn is
tagged with a dialogue act, each act carries a pressure rank, and the fitted
slope across the call plus the act-sequence likelihood ratio under scam and
benign Markov models become features. Bag-of-words cannot see order.

**Code-switching as a fraud signal.** Indian fraud scripts keep the money and
authority vocabulary in English while the threat itself is a Hindi verb
phrase. Per-token language identification yields a code-mixing index,
switch-point entropy, and the share of fraud entities carried by English.

Measured on the corpus, that last one discriminates hard but in the opposite
direction to the obvious guess: bank, OTP, payment, authority and
personal-information entities are 89 to 96 percent English in both classes,
while threat deadlines are only 23 percent English ("band ho jayega") and
appear only in scam calls. A scam call's fraud entities therefore come out
less English overall, 0.63 against 0.90. English marks the financial noun,
Hindi carries the coercion. Full table in `docs/REPORT.md` section 4.4.

**Time to detection.** Accuracy on a finished recording is the wrong question
for a system meant to interrupt a live call. The pipeline re-scores after every
turn and reports how many seconds of audio it needed before crossing the alert
threshold. It is a capability and a metric at the same time.

**CallForge.** A parametric generator produces code-mixed scam and benign
dialogues with gold BIO entities, gold dialogue acts and gold per-token
language tags, emitted by construction instead of annotated by hand, and
renders paired human-sounding and synthetic-sounding audio. The whole project
therefore reproduces on a laptop with no dataset downloads.

## Where the audio comes from

Three sources, each doing a job the others cannot. Keeping them separate is
deliberate: blending them into one headline number would hide which claim
rests on real speech and which rests on generated speech.

| source | what it is | what it is used for | licence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GramVaani**, OpenSLR 118 | 2,884 transcribed real Hindi **telephone** utterances, 7.6 hours, 2,726 speakers, 1,728 natively 8 kHz, with gender and accent labels | the bonafide side of the voice branch | free for academic use |
| **NCSU WSPR robocalls** | 1,432 genuine illegal robocalls with transcripts | out-of-domain test for the intent branch, real fraud scripts nothing was tuned on | public domain (FTC evidence) |
| **CallForge**, in this repo | generated Hinglish dialogues with gold BIO, acts and language tags, rendered with neural voices | the fusion grid and the live monitor, where audio and transcript have to match | MIT, with the code |

Both downloads are direct HTTP with no account and no approval form, which was
a hard constraint on this project. Fetch them with:

```bash
python -m swarkavach.datasets          # shows what is on disk
swarkavach fetch-data                  # downloads GramVaani and the robocalls
```

### The Hindi anti-spoofing set

No public Hindi anti-spoofing dataset exists. The survey behind this project
checked, and the nearest thing (IITG-HingCoS: 25 hours of Hinglish at 8 kHz
over the telephone) is not distributed at all. So the set is constructed here,
using the ASVspoof recipe: for each real GramVaani utterance, the spoof side
renders **the same transcript** with a neural voice, half of those additionally
through an LPC vocoder. Matched content is the point. If the two classes talked
about different things, a detector could separate them on topic and never learn
anything about voice.

### What the generated audio is, and is not

The corpus renderer uses real neural voices, so the audio says the words on the
screen. It is still machine generated on both sides of the human against cloned
axis, because nobody has recorded these scam scripts. The cloned cells are put
through a vocoder re-synthesis that imposes the low jitter, low shimmer and
raised harmonic-to-noise ratio that real cloning leaves behind.

So the local four-cell grid measures whether the method separates the cells,
not whether a machine can be told from a person. That second question is
answered on GramVaani against real TTS, and on ASVspoof and In-the-Wild in the
Colab notebooks. `docs/REPORT.md` section 4.2.1 spells this out.

## Quick start

Requires Python 3.10 or newer. No GPU, no ffmpeg, no dataset download.

```bash
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate            # Windows;  source .venv/bin/activate elsewhere
pip install -r requirements.txt
pip install -e .

swarkavach demo
```

`demo` generates a corpus, trains every model, runs the evaluation and opens
the console at <http://127.0.0.1:7860>. It takes a few minutes on a normal
laptop.

The steps individually:

```bash
swarkavach gen-corpus --n 480 --audio   # build the annotated corpus
swarkavach train                        # CRF, TF-IDF, anti-spoof, fusion
swarkavach evaluate                     # writes data/results/*.json
swarkavach serve                        # the console
swarkavach score data/corpus/audio/call_0007.wav
swarkavach info                         # what is loaded, what is missing
```

## The console

Five views, all offline, no CDN.

- **Live monitor.** Pick a call, press start, watch the transcript stream in
  turn by turn while the risk gauge moves and entities light up as they are
  detected. The moment the score crosses the alert threshold is marked on the
  waveform with its timestamp. There is a button that synthesises a brand new
  call on the spot so a demo is never a replay.
- **Call analysis.** One call, everything behind the verdict: exact Shapley
  contributions for the fusion features, the transcript with entity and
  lexicon spans highlighted, the prosody trace against the lexical pressure
  trace, spectrogram, and a voice-quality profile against corpus means.
- **Results lab.** The ablation grid, anti-spoof EER by feature set and model,
  DET curves, entity F1, intent accuracy, codec robustness, time-to-detection
  distribution and the calibration curve. Every chart ships with its table.
- **Corpus.** Cell counts, entity distribution, scenario mix, code-mixing
  statistics split by class, and a searchable call browser.
- **System.** Architecture, the novelty write-up, ethics and limits, the
  runtime state and the syllabus mapping.

## Layout

```
src/swarkavach/
  schema.py          Call, Turn, Verdict, the 25 fusion features, the tagset
  config.py          paths and every DSP constant
  audioio.py         WAV read and write, resampling, no librosa
  channel.py         G.711 companding in numpy, GSM and AMR through ffmpeg
  dsp/               framing, STFT, filterbanks, MFCC GFCC LFCC CQCC LPCC,
                     deltas, pitch and voice quality, VAD. All written by hand.
  corpus/            lexicon, template grammar, generator, source-filter synth
  text/              normalisation, language ID, POS, CRF from scratch,
                     BiLSTM tagger, intent models, coercion, ASR adapter
  antispoof/         pooled features, GMM, GBM, RawNet-lite, speaker embedding
  fusion/            PIM, featuriser, calibrated model, exact SHAP, streaming
  pipeline.py        the thing the server and the CLI talk to
  evaluate.py        every metric, writes data/results/*.json
server/              Flask API plus the console (vanilla JS, no build step)
notebooks/           Colab notebooks for the parts a laptop cannot do
docs/                contracts, annotation guidelines, ethics, report material
```

## What runs where

The laptop path is complete on its own. It uses the generated corpus, the
classical and small neural models, and CPU inference throughout.

Four things need a GPU and live in `notebooks/`:

| Notebook | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `01_setup_and_data.ipynb` | Pulls ASVspoof 2019 LA and the In-the-Wild set, checks them |
| `02_antispoof_train.ipynb` | Trains RawNet-lite on real spoofing data, exports the checkpoint |
| `03_asr_ner_intent.ipynb` | Whisper transcription with WER against gold, MuRIL intent fine-tune |
| `04_fusion_and_eval.ipynb` | Re-runs the fusion and ablation on the real-data scores |
| `05_voice_cloning.ipynb` | Consented XTTS cloning for the synthetic cells |

Each notebook writes JSON into `data/results/` with `"source": "colab"`, and
the console's provenance table shows which numbers came from which machine.
Nothing is hidden behind an unlabelled average.

## Ethics

This is a detector. Nothing here automates a scam or clones a stranger.

The offline audio comes from a source-filter synthesiser in this repository
and is not a clone of anybody. The Colab cloning notebook is restricted to a
speaker's own voice with written consent, and the audio is deleted after the
assessment. No real bank, no real UPI handle, no real phone number and no real
case number appears anywhere in the generated corpus.

The honest limitations are written up in `docs/ETHICS.md` and shown in the
console. The short version: offline numbers come from generated audio and
demonstrate that the method works rather than proving performance on real
calls, false positives on legitimate synthetic voices carry real cost, and
Hinglish transcription is the weakest link in the whole chain.
